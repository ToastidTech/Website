Business Automation: What Should You Automate First?

The best first automation is usually not the flashiest one. It is a repetitive workflow with clear rules, meaningful frequency, and a measurable payoff.

Look for repetition

Start with tasks people perform over and over: moving information between systems, sending routine follow-ups, generating standard documents, sorting requests, updating records, or preparing recurring reports.

Fix the process before automating it

If a workflow requires workarounds, duplicate data entry, unclear ownership, or frequent manual corrections, automate later. First simplify the steps and define what “done” means.

A simple prioritization framework

Evaluate each candidate by frequency, time consumed, error risk, business impact, and implementation effort. A task that happens daily and takes several minutes may deserve attention before a complex workflow that happens twice a year.

QuestionWhat to look for
FrequencyDoes this happen daily or weekly?
TimeHow much staff time does it consume?
RulesCan the steps be clearly defined?
RiskWhat happens if the automation makes a mistake?
ImpactDoes fixing it improve revenue, service, or capacity?

Automation with AI

AI can help when inputs are messy or language-heavy. Examples include classifying incoming requests, extracting information from documents, drafting responses, or summarizing notes. Important workflows should include appropriate review and fallback paths.

Build a roadmap, not a pile of bots

Document the workflow, owner, systems involved, expected outcome, and measurement before implementation. A roadmap keeps automation tied to business operations instead of turning the software stack into a collection of disconnected experiments.
