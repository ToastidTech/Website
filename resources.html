CopeIs my conversation with Cope private?
Your chats aren't shared, sold, or read by humans — Cope is designed to keep your conversations private. That said, it's still an internet-connected app, so treat it like any online service: no absolute guarantees, just a design that keeps your business yours.

AI News · Week of October 26, 2026

What happened in AI this week.

The week's AI headlines, translated for people who run businesses — what changed, and why it matters to you. Every story below is verified against its source. No rumor-mill headlines.

Regulation · Oct 19

DHS orders DeepSeek out of federal civilian IT — including tools built on it

On October 19, the Department of Homeland Security filed a notice — published in the Federal Register on October 20 — excluding products from Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd. and its affiliates from federal civilian agency procurements, and ordering removal of DeepSeek products and third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations from civilian agency IT.

Why it matters: This does not ban DeepSeek for private businesses, but it changes vendor vetting: if you sell to a federal civilian agency, or your tool quietly runs on a DeepSeek model, that dependency is now a procurement problem. Know what model sits underneath your stack before a customer asks.
Source: Federal Register →
Models · Oct 19

MiniMax ships M3.1 Flash, halving its own agent-model price

On October 19, MiniMax released M3.1 Flash, which it describes as an ultra-efficient open-weight model for everyday work. Its published API pricing is $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens, half the standard rates for MiniMax M3. MiniMax says agent-task costs fall about 70% versus the previous generation — a vendor claim, not an independent test.

Why it matters: Cheaper agent models lower the cost of running routine automation, but price per token is not cost per job. Test it on your own workflows, with human review, before letting a bargain model touch customer-facing work.
Source: MiniMax →
Tools · Oct 20

Google gives Gemini a small-business hub — brand, money, and your apps in one place

On October 20, Google announced a business landing page in the Gemini app, plus connections to Google Business Profile, Google Ads, and Google Workspace and third-party apps including Adobe, Canva, Dropbox, Shopify, Wix, Zoho, and Xero. Google says owners can save a brand style for on-brand visuals and turn spreadsheets, receipts, and purchase records into financial graphics and interactive models.

Why it matters: For a one-person shop, the win is not another chatbot — it is fewer copy-paste trips between the store, the books, and the ad account. Connect one app first, check what Gemini can actually change in it, and keep financial outputs verified before they go anywhere official.
Source: Google →
Agents · Oct 21

Anthropic's Claude Duet: an agent that comes to you — waitlist only, for now

On October 21, Anthropic opened a research-preview waitlist for Claude Duet, a standalone app where Claude works as a proactive agent for professionals and small business owners. Built around Claude Opus 5.5, it sends a morning brief across connected apps, keeps working in its own cloud virtual machine when you step away, and — Anthropic says — asks approval before sending messages, making payments, signing documents, or deleting anything.

Why it matters: This is the agent model shifting from "ask it a question" to "hand it the follow-up." That only works for a small business if approvals stay real: start with read-and-draft jobs like invoice reminders, not anything that moves money or messages customers unsupervised.
Source: Anthropic →
Commerce · Oct 21

ChatGPT Ads reach 89 countries as 100,000+ advertisers pile in

On October 21, OpenAI expanded ChatGPT advertising to 26 more countries, bringing the total to 89, Reuters reported. OpenAI says more than 100,000 advertisers have run campaigns since ads began testing in the US earlier this year; ads appear only on Free and Go plans, while Plus, Pro, and Enterprise stay ad-free.

Why it matters: ChatGPT is becoming another paid-discovery channel, not just a place people ask questions. If your customers search there, a small test budget in Ads Manager is now a legitimate experiment — but measure it like any ad channel, on leads and sales, not on novelty.
Source: Reuters →
Tools · Oct 22

OpenAI's Decisions API goes generally available at $0.10 per million input tokens

On October 22, OpenAI moved its Decisions API out of beta. It returns typed answers — a probability, a choice from a list you supply, or a score on a rubric — instead of free-form text, using gpt-6-luna, with up to 400 questions per request, native JSON input, and GPT-Live integration. Pricing is $0.10 per million non-cached input tokens and $0.01 cached, with no output-token charges, per OpenAI.

Why it matters: Most small-business automation is not writing essays — it is classifying, routing, and scoring: is this ticket urgent, which department owns it, how severe is this bug? A cheap, structured endpoint fits that work far better than paying a chat model to write a paragraph you then have to parse.
Source: OpenAI →
