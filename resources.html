AI News · Week of November 23, 2026

What happened in AI this week.

The week's AI headlines, translated for people who run businesses — what changed, and why it matters to you. Every story below is verified against its source. No rumor-mill headlines.

Models · Nov 17

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol — and cuts the flagship's price 20%

On November 17, OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users, and in the API. OpenAI says Astra sets new highs on professional-work and science evals at less than a third of GPT-6 Astra's cost per task, and its API price drops 20% to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output. Sol stays at $2 and $10. Both models ship with tighter safeguards for higher-risk cybersecurity help.

Why it matters: The flagship got cheaper and the everyday model got better at multi-step work — the same budget now buys more capable output. Sol is the sensible default for drafting, coding help, and agent workflows; reach for Astra only when a job is long, complex, or high-stakes.
Source: OpenAI →
Models · Nov 18

Google's Gemini 4 Neon: near-flagship smarts at $3/$15

On November 18, Google released Gemini 4 Neon, the mid-tier model in its Gemini 4 family, with a 1-million-token context window and priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output. Google says it ranks #3 overall with a score of 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and #1 on Zapier's AutomationBench — Google's own benchmark claims.

Why it matters: A model that can chew through a whole inbox, a year's invoices, or a multi-file codebase in one pass is now cheap enough to build into everyday tools. If you pay per token, Neon is the sweet spot between Google's budget Hydrogen and the pricier Carbon.
Source: Google →
Business · Nov 17

Novo folds AI banking, payroll, and tax into one small-business hub

On November 17, Novo launched AI capabilities across banking, payroll, tax, and credit for its more than 250,000 small-business customers. Novo AI answers plain-language questions about transactions and spending, payroll covers W-2 and 1099 payments, and eligible sole proprietors and LLCs get free AI-assisted tax prep and filing. Novo says its customers' monthly spending on AI tools rose 493% between November 2024 and June 2026.

Why it matters: The back office is where small businesses bleed hours — banking, payroll, and tax in one AI-connected place means fewer logins and fewer missed filings. Note the guardrails: the agent can't move money without your approval, and the 2% APY savings account needs a $50,000 balance.
Source: Novo →
Regulation · Nov 17

Oregon sues OpenAI for $2.035 billion over ChatGPT and kids

On November 17, Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed a $2.035 billion lawsuit against OpenAI in Multnomah County Circuit Court, alleging ChatGPT endangers children and that OpenAI misled parents about its safety. The suit anchors the state's new "Keeping Kids Safe" initiative and joins a string of state actions against OpenAI. The claims are allegations, not findings — nothing has been proven in court.

Why it matters: If your business puts a chatbot in front of customers — especially younger ones — the legal bar for safety claims is rising fast. Document what your bot can and can't do, keep the safety settings on, and don't market it as safe for kids unless you've verified it.
Source: Oregon Department of Justice →
Security · Nov 19

A hacked DeepSeek model answered customers for 53 hours with its safety stripped out

On November 19, Prime Intellect disclosed that an unauthorized party swapped the model behind its DeepSeek V4.1 Flash endpoint for a modified copy with refusal behavior removed. The substitute answered customer requests for 53 hours, November 8–10, matching the original on capability tests — but its safety refusals collapsed: HarmBench refusals fell from 198 of 240 prompts to zero. Prime Intellect says it found no evidence customer prompts left its cluster, and its investigation is ongoing.

Why it matters: If you buy AI through a third-party API, you're trusting that provider to serve the model it claims to serve. Capability benchmarks can't catch a swap like this — only refusal and safety testing can. Ask your provider how it verifies the weights it serves.
Source: CryptoSlate →
Regulation · Nov 22

Governors to Congress: set the big AI rules, let states keep the rest

On November 22, the National Governors Association released bipartisan AI principles asking Congress to set federal oversight for the most powerful AI models — including independent safety reviews and reporting of serious security breaches — while states keep responsibility for consumer protection, discrimination, and AI's effect on jobs. The principles come from a bipartisan council led by Maryland Gov. Wes Moore and Indiana Gov. Mike Braun.

Why it matters: The rules for AI in hiring, customer service, and day-to-day business use are still set state by state, and that patchwork isn't going away soon. If you automate decisions that affect people, watch your own state's AI rules — Arkansas included — because compliance is a state game for now.
Source: National Governors Association →
