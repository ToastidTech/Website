Business Technology Audit: Find the Friction Before You Buy More Software

A useful technology audit looks beyond the software list. It examines how people, processes, systems, data, and customers interact.

What should a business audit examine?

Start with five connected areas: people, process, technology, data, and customer experience. A problem in one area often creates symptoms in another.

1. People

Identify who owns each important workflow, where knowledge is concentrated in one person, and where staff spend time on repetitive or avoidable work.

2. Process

Map the real process, not the process everyone assumes exists. Look for duplicate entry, unnecessary approvals, handoffs, bottlenecks, and manual workarounds.

3. Technology

Inventory the systems in use and ask what each one actually contributes. Check for overlapping tools, abandoned subscriptions, integration gaps, access issues, and capabilities the business is paying for but not using.

4. Data

Follow important information from capture to storage to reporting. Look for duplicate records, inconsistent definitions, missing ownership, and manual transfers between systems.

5. Customer experience

Trace the customer's journey from first contact through delivery and follow-up. Technology should reduce friction for the customer, not merely create a more elaborate back office.

Turn findings into decisions

An audit is most useful when findings become a prioritized action plan. Separate quick fixes from larger projects, document dependencies, and identify what should not be changed yet.
