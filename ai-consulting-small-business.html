AI Consulting for Small Business: Start With the Problem

AI can be useful when it reduces repetitive work, improves access to information, or helps people make decisions. It becomes expensive noise when the business problem was never defined.

Find the work worth improving

Start by listing repetitive tasks, slow handoffs, common questions, document-heavy work, and places where people repeatedly copy information from one system to another. These are candidates for investigation, not automatic invitations to add AI.

AI is not the same as automation

Automation follows known rules. AI can handle less predictable inputs such as natural language, images, or unstructured documents. A useful system may combine both: AI interprets the input, then automation moves the result through a defined workflow.

Build, buy, or automate?

Existing software is often the simplest answer. Automation can connect tools when the workflow is already clear. Custom development becomes more relevant when the business has a differentiated workflow, product, or requirement that off-the-shelf software cannot handle well.

Questions to answer before deployment

  • What business outcome are we trying to improve?
  • What information will the system receive?
  • What happens when the AI is wrong or uncertain?
  • Who reviews important outputs?
  • How will success be measured?
  • What data should never be sent to the system?

Keep the first version small

A focused pilot is easier to test, measure, secure, and improve. Pick one workflow, establish a baseline, and learn from real usage before expanding the system.
